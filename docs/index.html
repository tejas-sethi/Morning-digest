Accepting free will doesn't exist could make us less hateful and ashamed
The Conversation AU
New psychology research suggests that embracing a deterministic view of human behaviour — the idea that our choices are shaped by genetics, upbringing and circumstance rather than pure autonomous will — is associated with reduced shame, self-blame and hatred toward others. Crucially, the research finds that ditching belief in free will does not erode our sense of personal accountability, challenging the widespread assumption that moral responsibility requires the existence of free will. It's a thought-provoking finding with real implications for how therapeutic and behaviour-support frameworks, including those used in disability and mental health contexts, might approach blame and motivation.
