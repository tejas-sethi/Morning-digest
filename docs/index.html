iPhone 18 won't debut this fall, here's why
9to5Mac
Apple is breaking with its long-standing tradition of releasing the full iPhone lineup simultaneously this September, with only the Pro models and the much-anticipated foldable iPhone set to land in the usual autumn window. The standard iPhone 18 will follow later, a significant departure that signals Apple is prioritising its most premium — and most profitable — devices first. If you've been holding out for the base model, you may need to sit on your hands a little longer.
Businesses sue Trump as he defends 'very standard tariffs'
ABC News Business
Two small American businesses have taken the extraordinary step of suing the US president in an attempt to halt sweeping new import tariffs covering dozens of countries, including Australia. Trump has brushed off the legal challenge, describing the levies as routine trade policy, but the case raises serious questions about the limits of executive power over trade. For Australian exporters already navigating a choppy global environment, the outcome will be worth watching closely.
Ebola deaths in DRC surge past 1,300 as virus 'spreading like a wildfire'
Al Jazeera
The Ebola outbreak in the Democratic Republic of Congo has now claimed more than 1,300 lives, with officials warning the virus is moving faster than any previously recorded outbreak and could surpass the worst episodes in history. Health workers are struggling to contain the spread in a region already destabilised by conflict and limited infrastructure. The story is a sobering reminder that the world's attention — and resources — are urgently needed beyond the headlines that dominate Western news cycles. This challenges the comfortable assumption that major Ebola outbreaks are a contained, manageable problem of the past; the speed and scale here suggest the international response frameworks built after 2014 may be dangerously insufficient.
Couple told two modular homes on one block makes it an 'estate'
ABC News Business
An Illawarra couple spent two years and considerable goodwill trying to add a second modular home to their own block, only to be solemnly informed by authorities that two homes equals a 'manufactured housing estate' — a classification presumably invented to stop something far grander than a family wanting a granny flat. It is the kind of bureaucratic logic that makes you wonder if the planning rulebook was written by someone who has never tried to build anything. Australia's housing crisis, meet its planning crisis.

Jurgen Klopp has wasted absolutely no time making his presence felt as Germany head coach, opening his tenure not with a rousing tactical manifesto but with a pointed warning to the press: stay out of his family's life or he walks. Only Klopp could turn a first day on the job into a cliffhanger. Germany fans will be hoping the drama stays firmly off the pitch once the actual football begins.
