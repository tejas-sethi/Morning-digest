Older people open to downsizing but facing barriers
ABC News Business
More than half of Australians over 55 say they're open to downsizing, but research identifies significant structural and emotional barriers preventing them from making the move. This matters for Melbourne buyers: if downsizers can't move, larger family homes stay locked out of the market longer, tightening supply further. Understanding what's clogging the pipeline helps frame what you're competing against heading into October.

More European FAs withdraw support for Infantino as pressure intensifies
The Guardian Football
The Football Association, Wales, and Serbia have joined Finland in withdrawing their backing for Gianni Infantino's re-election as FIFA president, with UEFA now threatening legal action over his failed plan to sell a stake in future World Cup revenues to private investors. The revolt is accelerating quickly, with senior UEFA sources describing threats of 'non co-operation' with FIFA if Infantino doesn't step down. This is a significant governance story for world football with real implications for how future World Cups — including the next cycle — are structured and funded.
New study shows there's a growing constituency reshaping Australian politics: renters
The Conversation AU
Analysing more than 25 years of voting data, this research shows renters are now a large and politically distinct bloc whose preferences are increasingly diverging from homeowners — and whose numbers are growing fast enough to shift electoral outcomes. The political pressure this creates may ultimately be the strongest driver of housing policy reform, more so than any individual government program. This challenges the assumption that housing policy is primarily shaped by homeowner interests and suggests the window for buyer-friendly policy could actually be widening.
